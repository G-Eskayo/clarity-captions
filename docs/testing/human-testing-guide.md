# Seal: human testing guide

For anyone trying the app and telling us what happened. No technical knowledge needed. You can't
break anything, and there are no wrong answers: we want to know exactly what you experienced.

## What Seal does

It listens to the people around you and shows what they say as text on the screen, and it starts
a new line when the voice changes ("Speaker 1", "Speaker 2"). It can mix up similar voices. Everything happens on your phone. Nothing is sent
anywhere.

## Before you start (about 5 minutes)

1. Install Seal from the TestFlight invitation we send you.
2. Open it. The first time, it walks you through a few short setup screens. Tap the big button on
   each one. If any screen confuses you, **that is the most useful thing to tell us.**
3. Allow the microphone when asked.

## The basic test (always do this one)

1. Take the phone out and open Seal.
2. Tap **Start captions**.
3. Set the phone on the table or counter where people are talking.
4. Talk normally for about 5 minutes. Don't speak for the app.
5. Read the captions as you go. Tap the round **X** at the top right when you're done.

## Situations to try (as many as you can, at least two)

| # | Situation | What to watch for |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | A quiet room, two people talking | Does it keep up? Are the words right? Does it tell you apart? |
| 2 | A restaurant or café table, 2 to 5 people | Does background noise show up as words? Are the right people labeled? |
| 3 | A group around a table, people interrupting each other | Does it keep up when voices overlap? |
| 4 | The phone in a different spot: pocket edge, far end of the table, on a counter | How far away can someone be and still show up? |
| 5 | Someone talking fast, or softly, or with an accent | What gets missed or wrong? |

## What to notice

- **Speed:** how long after someone speaks do the words appear? Instant, a little late, or too late to follow?
- **Keeping up:** does it ever fall behind or stop?
- **Accuracy:** how many words are wrong or missing? Which kinds (names, short words, mumbled words)?
- **Who's who:** do the speaker labels follow the right person? Do they mix people up?
- **Distance:** whose words show up and whose don't?
- **Reading comfort:** is the text big and clear enough? Do the lines break in sensible places? Does it scroll well?
- **Confusion:** anything you didn't understand how to do, or tapped by mistake.

## After each session, answer these (a few minutes)

Rate 1 (very bad) to 5 (very good):

1. Speed of the captions: ___
2. How well it kept up: ___
3. Accuracy of the words: ___
4. Telling people apart: ___
5. Easy to read: ___
6. Easy to use with no instructions: ___

Then, in your own words:

- The best moment:
- The worst moment:
- Anything you would change first:
- Would you use this in real life? Why or why not?

## If something goes wrong

Note roughly what time it happened, what you were doing, and what you saw on the screen, then send
us a screenshot or a short description. A red "Stopped" message is worth a screenshot.

## Privacy

Seal does not record or send your conversations. If a facilitator asks to save a test session for
analysis, that is separate: it needs your clear yes first, stays on the device, and can be deleted
after. **Always tell the other people at the table that you're testing a captioning app.**

## For the person running the test (facilitator notes)

- Do the basic test yourself first, so you know what good looks like.
- Let the tester struggle a little before helping, and write down where they hesitate. Don't
  explain the buttons; if they need explaining, the app has to change.
- Match the "primary environments": restaurants, the dinner table, sit-down conversation.
- Record: situation, number of people, noise level, distance, and the ratings above.
- Results go back into the restaurant-conditions test and the usability test issues.
