How it works

One big button. Nothing to set up each time.

1

Put your phone on the table

Open Seal Speak and tap Start captions.

2

Read along

Words appear as people talk. When the voice changes, Seal starts a new line and labels it Speaker 1, Speaker 2. Sounds like laughter or a doorbell show up as short labels.

3

Pause, save or start fresh

Tap ✕ to pause. [ Save ] keeps the conversation on your phone for 30 days; [ New ] starts a clean page.

Private by design

A conversation holds other people's words. They stay on your phone.

  • Captions are made on your device. Audio is never recorded or sent anywhere.
  • Works with no internet connection after a one-time setup, including in airplane mode.
  • No account, no ads, no tracking.
  • Saved conversations stay on your phone for 30 days, and you can delete any of them sooner. Read the privacy policy.

Made to be easy to read

Large text

Follows your phone's text size, with A− and A+ to go bigger or smaller.

Six colour themes

Three light and three dark. Every one keeps strong contrast between the words and the background.

Six letterings

OpenDyslexic, Atkinson Hyperlegible, Standard, Rounded, Serif and Typewriter. Pick whichever is most comfortable.

Why Seal looks and works this way

The choices behind the design, and where they come from.

Contrast between light and dark matters most

Readers with low vision read better with strong brightness contrast than with colour contrast alone. So every theme is built for brightness contrast, and a test makes sure each one reaches at least 7:1.

Source: Legge et al. (1990), Psychophysics of reading XI.

You choose the size, lettering and colours

Caption size, lettering, spacing and the contrast between caption and background all affect how easy captions are to read. Seal lets you change the size, the lettering and the colours.

Source: a caption readability study with older TV viewers, Sun Repository.

Light and dark, offered equally

Research found dark text on a light background read a little better, with a smaller difference for older adults, and dark mode caused no extra eye strain. Both work, so Seal offers three of each instead of choosing for you.

Source: Piepenbrock et al. (2013), summarised by the Nielsen Norman Group. The study was with people with healthy eyes.

OpenDyslexic, offered for comfort

Seal's default lettering is OpenDyslexic. Studies haven't shown it makes reading faster or more accurate, so it's offered because some people find it comfortable, alongside Atkinson Hyperlegible (designed by the Braille Institute for low vision) and four others.

Source: Wery & Diliberto (2017), Annals of Dyslexia.

Built on proven on-device tools

Speech-to-text uses Apple's on-device speech recognition. Telling voices apart uses the open-source FluidAudio library and NVIDIA's Sortformer model, bundled in the app so nothing goes over the network.

Good to know

  • Captions are made by a computer and can contain mistakes, names most of all. For anything important, check with the person speaking.
  • Seal goes by the sound of the voice, so it can mix people up, especially voices that sound alike. It doesn't know who anyone is.
  • Captions are in English for now. Seal needs an iPhone or iPad with iOS 26 or later.
  • Seal Speak is not a medical device.
