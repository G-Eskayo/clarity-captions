What it does

One screen and one button to start. Nothing to set up each time.

1

Put your phone on the table

Open Seal Speak and tap Start captions. Words show up as people talk.

2

Read along

When the voice changes, Seal starts a new line and labels it Speaker 1 or Speaker 2. Sounds like laughter or a doorbell show up as short labels.

3

Pause when you need to

Tap ✕ and the words dim, with [ Save ] and [ New ] on top. Tap the dim to scroll back and reread. Hold an empty spot to bring the buttons back.

Copy a few words

Hold a word to select it, drag the handles to take in more, then tap [ Copy ]. Paste it into a message or a note.

Keep a conversation

Tap [ Save ]. Your saved conversations are behind the list icon at the top right. Each one stays on your phone for 30 days, then it's deleted.

Share it if you want

Open a saved conversation and tap [ Share ] to send the text to Notes, Messages, Mail, Files or AirDrop. [ Delete ] gets rid of it now.

Why Seal looks and works this way

The choices behind the design, and where they come from.

Contrast between light and dark matters most

Readers with low vision read better with strong brightness contrast than with color contrast alone. So every theme is built for brightness contrast, and a test makes sure each one reaches at least 7:1.

Source: Legge et al. (1990), Psychophysics of reading XI.

You choose the size, lettering and colors

Caption size, lettering, spacing and the contrast between caption and background all affect how easy captions are to read. Seal lets you change the size, the lettering and the colors.

Source: a caption readability study with older TV viewers, Sun Repository.

Light and dark, offered equally

Research found dark text on a light background read a little better, with a smaller difference for older adults, and dark mode caused no extra eye strain. Both work, so Seal offers three of each instead of choosing for you.

Source: Piepenbrock et al. (2013), summarised by the Nielsen Norman Group. The study was with people with healthy eyes.

OpenDyslexic, offered for comfort

Seal's default lettering is OpenDyslexic. Studies haven't shown it makes reading faster or more accurate, so it's offered because some people find it comfortable, alongside Atkinson Hyperlegible (designed by the Braille Institute for low vision) and four others.

Source: Wery & Diliberto (2017), Annals of Dyslexia.

Built on proven on-device tools

Speech-to-text uses Apple's on-device speech recognition. Telling voices apart uses the open-source FluidAudio library and NVIDIA's Sortformer model, bundled in the app so nothing goes over the network.

For the curious: how we tested

Captions in English

  • Recordings: the EdAcc corpus, 31.4 hours of conversation in many English accents, checked by people (CC BY-SA). 17,528 clips from 122 speakers.
  • Every engine ran on exactly the same clips. A mistake is the standard word error rate: words that were wrong, missing or added, out of all the words said.
  • Each score has a 95% confidence interval from a speaker-level bootstrap. When two ranges overlap, we don't call a winner.
  • Timing: 526 clips (45 minutes) fed in as if live. "Correct word" is when a word first shows correctly, measured from when it was said.

Questions

What does it cost?

$4.99, once. No subscription, no in-app purchases, no ads.

Does it need the internet?

Only once. The first time you start, your iPhone may download Apple's speech files. After that it captions with no connection at all, airplane mode included.

Does it record or upload conversations?

No. Sound is turned into text on your phone and the audio isn't kept. Text is only kept if you tap [ Save ], and it stays on your phone.

Which phones does it work on?

iPhone 12 or newer with iOS 26 or later. It's iPhone only for now, and captions are in English.

Does it recognize people?

No. Seal goes by the sound of the voice and labels lines Speaker 1 and Speaker 2. It can mix people up, especially voices that sound alike, and it doesn't know anyone's name.

How accurate are the captions?

Captions are made by a computer and can contain mistakes, names most of all. For anything important, check with the person speaking.

How long are saved conversations kept?

30 days, then they're deleted on their own. They aren't backed up to iCloud. Open one from the list icon and tap [ Delete ] to remove it sooner.

What if I forget to stop it?

Seal stops by itself after 5 minutes with no one talking. In Settings you can change that to 15 or 30 minutes, or never.

Is Seal Speak a medical device?

No. Seal Speak is not a medical device. It's an app that shows conversation as text.
